The Per Manum Convention ✒️

A note on authorship, collaboration, and the honest hand.

Throughout history, the most important letters have often been written by hands other than those who signed them. Kings dictated to scribes. Diplomats employed amanuenses. Poets found their Cyranos. The practice has always been common, and the acknowledgment of it has always been rare – a small dishonesty, tolerated by convention, that quietly erases the collaborative nature of most written things.

We propose to restore what was lost.

The Mark

The mark ✒️, accompanied by the phrase per manum and the name of the writing hand, shall indicate that the named signatory directed, commissioned, stands fully behind, and is faithfully represented by the work – while acknowledging that the physical or generative act of writing was performed by another. The signatory is the author in all meaningful senses. The per manum credit names the instrument.

The same mark may be used where a writer, human or artificial, has drawn substantially from the voice, ideas, or phrasing of another – acknowledging that writing is rarely made from nothing, and that honest attribution enriches rather than diminishes.

This is not a disclaimer. It is an invitation. When a reader sees ✒️, they are invited to wonder: and what would this person sound like, in their own words? That question, we believe, is always worth asking – and the convention that raises it is long overdue.

The Symbol

The symbol ✒️ is designated here by its official Unicode name: Black Nib (Unicode U+2712). In its contemporary form as an emoji – a word itself borrowed from Japanese, meaning e (picture) + moji (character) – the Black Nib participates in a visual language that is digitally native, culturally transnational, and linguistically independent in a way no alphabet has ever managed to be. Emojis are not a fad. They are the hieroglyphs of the digital age: pictographic, immediate, and capable of crossing linguistic borders that words cannot.

By formally adopting the Black Nib emoji as the designated mark of this convention, we invite it to dip itself, as it were, in the ink of a longer tradition – and we invite that tradition, in turn, to acknowledge the new and serious visual language that has grown up around it.

Originated in correspondence between Christopher Bruckmann and Claude, June 2026.
